This page sorts every eight-instruction Turing machine in Tree Normal Form by shape, the number of states and symbols it uses, so that each cell can be settled on its own. Our own program runs a cell and a second, independent check confirms the result. Every colour, count and statement here comes from that calculation alone. A † marks what others found.

BBi(8) is attained in one of these 20 cells. They fit in a box of 2–6 states by 2–6 symbols.

Our longest checked run is 9,757,251 steps, in 3×4. It falls short of the longest known run. Nick Drozd found the longest known run, more than 6.889 × 101,565 steps†, in 2025, as the bbchallenge wiki records.

The census

The census counts 15,561,793,526 eight-instruction machines, each the child of a machine that halted within 4,000,000 steps. It left 486,204 shorter machines undecided, 14 with four instructions, 424 with five, 13,216 with six and 472,550 with seven, and machines grown from them are not counted, which is what a + marks. So there are at least 15,561,793,526.

What counts

A halter is run to its halt, a non-halter carries a cycler or translated-cycler certificate (Coq-BB5's Loop decider), an n-gram CPS certificate, a repeated-word-list certificate or a finite-automata-reduction certificate, and any other machine stays a holdout. A cell shows as open or resolved only once a second program, written from CHECKING.md alone, agrees on its count, every halt and every certificate. The results are informal, in the community's sense. A † marks a number credited to others. It is shown as their claim and not checked here.

Reproducing a cell

Anyone can check a cell whose page posts its claims file without trusting us. The checker is one Python file written from CHECKING.md, and it reads a cell's files and the census.

python bbi9check.py <a cell's directory> --census census.tsv

Each cell's page names the version of our producer that made its claims, and the checker verifies them without it.

  • Allen Brady's Tree Normal Form generates the machines.
  • Coq-BB5 proves the cycler and translated-cycler method in its Loop decider. Its NGramCPS decider is the model for n-gram CPS, and its RepWL decider, in spirit, for repeated word lists.
  • The bbchallenge deciders paper and Justin Blanchard's decider describe finite automata reduction, which Coq-BB5 verifies in Verifier_FAR.
  • The bbchallenge wiki's BBi tables give the counts the census reproduces.
