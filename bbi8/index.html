Why machines 7 and 8 never halt

#7  1RB1LC2LA---_2RC---------_0LA2RC3LC1RC
#8  1RB---2LC3LA_2LC---------_0LA2RC3RC1LC

Two of the last undecided machines with 8 instructions, from Nick Drozd's list. Machine 8 is machine 7 with its symbols relabelled, apart from two transitions.

  1. The tape holds a number y. Each bounce changes y by one rule, and y only grows.
  2. The machine halts only if y is a 1 followed by zeros in binary.
  3. The rule makes trailing zeros too slowly. A halting y that ends in m zeros would be exactly 2m, but every y that ends in m zeros is at least (2m − 1) × 2m.

Every number below is computed live. The tape comes from running the machine.

Step 1

The tape is a number

Machine 7 sweeps over a word of 1s and 2s. At each bounce off the right end, read the word in binary with 2 as one and 1 as zero, then add 1. That is y. Machine 8 uses 3 as one and 2 as zero. Between bounces, y follows one rule.

new y = y − 1 + 4 × 2z   where z is the number of zeros at the end of y in binary
2 is the digit one, 1 is the digit zero.

Step 2

Halting needs a round number

The machine halts only on a word of all ones. Adding 1 gives a 1 followed by zeros, like 100 or 10000. That is a power of 2, and 2m ends in exactly m zeros. So to halt, a y that ends in m zeros must be 2m itself, the smallest number ending in m zeros.

Word lengths are even, so the halting values are 4, 16, 64, … Step 3 rules out every power of 2.

Step 3

The rule makes zeros too slowly

Start from 2 and look at every y that ends in m zeros. The smallest one is (2m − 1) × 2m, which is 2m − 1 times the halting value 2m. So no y that ends in m zeros is 2m. That holds for every m, so y is never a power of 2.

y only grows, so the smallest y ending in m zeros is also the first one. The machine never halts.

Why step 3 never breaks

The rule looks the same at every scale. From any multiple Q × 2m, the next multiple of 2m is always (h(Q) + 2m) × 2m, where h is the rule. Try it.

Two facts drive this. On an odd number the rule adds 3. On 2Q it gives twice the rule on Q, plus 1. So one step at scale m + 1 is two steps at scale m, and the formula follows by induction.

Take Q = 2m − 1, which is odd. From (2m − 1) × 2m the next multiple of 2m is (2m + 1) × 2m+1, the next row of the table. Nothing in between ends in m zeros, so each row forces the next.
